# Reviews: doing it again, cold

Once you've shown you can do something, it comes back — a few days later, then a week or two,
then a month or two — to see whether you still can. [The overall guide](../learn/howto.md)
covers where reviews sit; this is what one is like from the inside.

## Why this exists at all

Everyone skips this part, and everyone regrets it: _I knew that once._

The spacing is doing real work. Coming back to something just as you're starting to lose it is
what makes it stick; coming back while it's still fresh teaches you almost nothing. That's why
the gaps get longer each time rather than staying fixed. (It's also a time saver; you don't
spend time reviewing things that are too easy.)

## What it's like

**A review sitting sweeps everything.** Not one topic — whatever is due, wherever it lives.
Three words from one topic and one capability from another is an ordinary list.

**You get the task and no help.** This is the one part of the whole process that withholds
anything, and it may feel unfriendly the first time. Ask and the tutor will say plainly that
this one is meant to be cold and that it'll go through it with you straight afterwards.

**If you ask anyway, you get the help.** Say so twice and it's yours. It's recorded as a miss,
which brings the item back sooner, and that is the whole consequence.

**The moment the verdict is in, the no-help constraint lifts** — whether you get it right or
wrong, a quick post-mortem conversation with the tutor that deepends your understanding will
also help long-term retention.

## A miss doesn't take away that you learned it previously

It's OK if you fail on the review. It's a lot easier to relearn than to learn it the first
time, and relearning just at the point of forgetting is the best way to cement long-term
retention.

## Two things that will look odd

**A review can arrive while you're still studying the same topic.** Reviews are per goal, not
per topic. Something you nailed in week one starts coming back three days later, while the rest
of that topic is still in progress. That's working as intended, not a bug.

**Nothing is graded and nothing reaches an instructor.** So a visible miss costs you exactly
nothing, which is what makes it worth recording honestly. The record is only useful to you if
it is true.

## Things to say

All the [thought partner moves](../thought-partner-moves.md) apply. These are particular to a
review:

**"I don't remember this at all."** Fine, and worth saying rather than guessing at it. It's a
miss either way; saying so gets you to the explanation faster. You might want to sneak a peak
at your `notes.md` file for the topic to see if that helps reorient you.

**"Can we do the rest another time?"** Yes. Stop whenever. What you don't get to stays due, and
nothing penalizes a short sitting.

**"Why is this back so soon?"** Usually because the last one was a miss, but maybe something
has gone wrong. Ask and it can tell you which.

**"I don't think I need this any more."** A real answer, and better said than silently ignored.
You can ask for the goal to be retired.
