# Words: the cheap half of learning something

**Status: draft.** Written 2026-08-23. Not yet used with students.

A lot of what makes a field hard isn't the ideas — it's that everyone talking about it uses
twenty words you don't have. This is how those get handled. It's deliberately the lightest part
of the whole process.

## A word is a goal, like anything else

It sits in `goals.md` with everything else you want. What's different is that it's **cheap**:
adding one is a line, and it doesn't reopen any conversation about your goals.

**So add them freely.** Hitting a word you don't have is the commonest way a topic grows, and
noticing that is a skill worth keeping. Say _"I keep seeing 'idempotent' and I don't know what
it means"_ and it gets written down. That's the whole transaction — no interview, no criteria
to agree, and nobody will explain it at you on the spot.

You'll also start with some. A topic the professor creates usually ships with a list of the
words that come with the territory.

## How a word goal gets met

Every word answers to the same one-sentence standard: **you use it well — correctly, and in a
way that shows you're not reading it off a definition.**

There are six ways you might be asked to show that. You don't pick; the tutor does, and it
picks something you haven't had recently.

|                 | what you do                                                               |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **DEFINE**      | say what it means, in your own words, nothing in front of you             |
| **INTERPRET**   | read a sentence you haven't seen that uses it, say what's being claimed   |
| **DISTINGUISH** | say what separates it from something it gets confused with                |
| **CATCH**       | find what's wrong in a sentence that misuses it                           |
| **APPLY**       | use it about something that actually happened in your own work            |
| **LOCATE**      | say what situation you'd be in where not having this word would block you |

In class, you'll also play a game, sort of like Taboo or Catch Phrase, where you have to
describe a concept and get your classmates to say the word.

**Doing any of the last four marks a word as done.** After that, it will come back for
[review](../review/howto.md) like anything else.

## Why the first two don't count

DEFINE and INTERPRET can both be passed from having been told the word ten seconds earlier. The
other four can't: each needs something _you_ bring — a distinction you drew, an error you
spotted, your own work, your own situation.

So a word is finished by one of the bottom four. **The top two are still worth doing** and get
recorded; they just don't close it out.

## LOCATE is the one worth sitting with

_What situation would you be in where not having this word would block you?_

It's the strangest of the six and it's the one that tells you whether a word is worth having at
all. A word you can define but can't place is a word you'll never reach for. If you genuinely
can't answer, that's real information — sometimes about you, sometimes about the word.

## Things to say

**"I've got this one."** If a word has landed and the checks feel like theatre, say so and it's
recorded as done — marked as your own say-so rather than as something you were checked on,
which is the honest way to record it. Use it when you mean it.

**"I don't think this word matters."** Also fine, and you can say it at any point — including
halfway through being checked on it. The word gets retired: it stops being offered and it stops
coming back for review, and it leaves the count, so `vocabulary 7/12` becomes `7/11` rather
than a twelfth you can never reach.

If you'd never attempted it, that's the end of it. If you had, those attempts stay in the
record — they happened — and the word simply shows as retired underneath the ones you met, with
whatever reason you gave. Nothing you did gets deleted.

Reversible, like retiring a topic. Say you want it back and it comes back, overdue if its
review date passed while it was out.

**"Give me a different task for this word."** If a move doesn't fit the word — DISTINGUISH
needs something to distinguish it _from_, and not every word has one — say so.
