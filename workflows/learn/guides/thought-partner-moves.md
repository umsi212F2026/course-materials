# Moves for any conversation with a thought partner

**Status: draft.** Written 2026-08-16. Not yet used with students.

There are some ways that you can get an agent (or a person) to be a better thought partner for
you. None of these are specific to any one task — they work in a conversation about anything,
and they're worth having to hand whenever you're working something out with an agent rather
than just asking it for an answer.

**"Please suggest several possibilities."** If you don't know where to start, ask for more than
one option.

**"Slow down. Let's take it one step at a time."**

**"Let's back up."** If the agent is treating something as a constraint or a settled decision
that is driving things in the wrong direction, try to identify that. Other variations: "I think
we're putting band-aids on; let's find the root cause", "Let's rethink", "Let's reconsider some
underlying assumptions."

**"Why do you think that?"** Ask for evidence, or the line of reasoning.

**"Why might this be a bad idea?"** Other variations: "What could go wrong?", "What are
arguments that an opponent might make?", "Critique this proposal", "Try to talk me out of
this".

**"Let me check my understanding. Are you saying..."** Then paraphrase, or ask about an
implication that you've inferred.

**"That's not what I meant. Perhaps we have a misunderstanding."** Often followed by one side
or the other doing a "check my understanding" move.
