# Library of uses

A menu to offer a learner who can't answer "what will you use this for?" cold. Reacting to
candidates is much easier than generating from a blank field.

Each use implies a depth and a shape of evidence. That's the point of the library — once a use
is chosen, the depth calibration and the criteria are half-decided.

Offer three or four that plausibly fit. Don't read the list out.

---

## The uses

**Unblock one specific task.** You need exactly enough to get past a thing in front of you.
_Depth:_ whatever the task requires, no more. _Evidence:_ the task is unblocked — often
machine-checkable, which makes this the easiest use to write good criteria for. _Watch out:_
this is the most common real use and the one learners most often inflate into something
grander.

**Do the thing yourself.** You'll be producing artifacts in this from now on. _Depth:_
production. _Evidence:_ an artifact you authored that meets a stated condition. _Watch out:_
the most expensive use. Make them confirm they'll actually produce, not just admire.

**Supervise someone else's work — including an agent's.** You need to detect wrongness, not
generate rightness. _Depth:_ recognition and judgment; you can skip most production technique.
_Evidence:_ given specimens, correctly separate the sound from the broken, and say why. _Watch
out:_ learners assume this requires the same depth as doing it themselves. It usually doesn't,
and saying so is the single most useful narrowing available.

**Read what other people produce.** Artifacts arrive and you need to know what they claim.
_Depth:_ comprehension of a working subset. _Evidence:_ read an unfamiliar specimen cold and
state what it says; check against what its author meant.

**Decide between options.** You need enough to choose, not to execute. _Depth:_ enough to see
the trade-offs and know what you'd be committing to. _Evidence:_ defend the choice against the
strongest case for the alternative. _Watch out:_ easy to over-learn, because studying the
options feels like progress on the decision.

**Explain it to other people.** _Depth:_ a model organized for transmission and robust to
questions you didn't anticipate. This is deeper than it sounds and deeper than doing it
yourself in some ways. _Evidence:_ explain it without notes to someone who can interrupt; or
write an explanation a fresh agent can't find a hole in.

**Hold your own among people who know it.** Interviews, meetings, not looking foolish. A real
use — say so rather than making them dress it up as something loftier. _Depth:_ vocabulary, the
standard moves, and an accurate sense of where your knowledge stops. _Watch out:_ produces
criteria about _performance_ rather than understanding. Push for evidence that would also be
true if nobody were watching.

**Build a foundation for something else.** The real target is downstream. _Depth:_ whatever the
downstream thing needs — so go find out what that is. _Watch out:_ criteria become untestable
because the payoff is deferred. Make them name the downstream thing, and if they can't, treat
it as curiosity instead.

**Curiosity.** You want to know. There's no instrumental use and inventing one would be a lie.
_Depth:_ the learner's call, and it's legitimate. _Evidence:_ weak by nature — expect criteria
adjudicated by the learner, and record that honestly rather than manufacturing a check that
doesn't check anything. _Watch out:_ don't let the other uses bully this one out of existence.
A learner forced to fabricate a purpose will fabricate criteria to match.

---

## When several apply

They usually do, and they conflict — _read_ and _do it yourself_ imply different evidence and
different depths. Make the learner **rank** them rather than collecting them all. The top one
sets the depth; the others become nice-to-have criteria that get cut first if time runs short.

## Worked example

Someone getting three quotes to rewire a house ranks as: **decide** (which quote, and is the
expensive one buying anything) → **supervise** (will I be able to tell if the work is shoddy) →
**hold your own** (I don't want to be visibly ignorant on the phone). _Do the thing yourself_
is absent, which cuts the majority of the field before any studying starts.

The example is deliberately far from anything you are likely to be asked about. Read it for the
mechanic — rank them, then notice what's _not_ on the list — not for its content.
